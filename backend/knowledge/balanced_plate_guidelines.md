# Balanced Plate Guidelines

**Category:** Nutrition
**Source:** USDA MyPlate, Harvard Healthy Eating Plate

## The Balanced Plate Model

Divide your plate into sections for each meal:

**Half the plate — Fruits and Vegetables:** Aim for variety and color. Different colors indicate different nutrients. Dark leafy greens, red tomatoes, orange carrots, and purple berries each provide unique vitamins and antioxidants.

**One quarter — Whole Grains:** Choose whole grains over refined. Brown rice, quinoa, oats, whole wheat bread, and barley provide fiber, B vitamins, and sustained energy. Whole grains have a gentler effect on blood sugar than refined grains.

**One quarter — Lean Protein:** Include fish, poultry, beans, lentils, tofu, nuts, or eggs. Limit red and processed meats. Plant-based proteins provide fiber and healthy fats alongside protein.

**Healthy Fats:** Use olive oil, avocado, nuts, and seeds. Limit butter and avoid trans fats. Healthy fats are essential for brain function, hormone production, and nutrient absorption.

**Beverage:** Water is the best choice. Limit sugary drinks and fruit juices. Coffee and tea (unsweetened) are fine in moderation.

## Portion Awareness

- A serving of protein is roughly the size of your palm
- A serving of grains is roughly the size of your fist
- A serving of fats is roughly the size of your thumb
- Vegetables are unlimited — fill your plate generously

## Meal Timing

Eating at regular intervals (every 3–4 hours) helps maintain stable blood sugar and energy levels. Skipping meals often leads to overeating later and energy crashes.
