# Understanding Sleep Cycles

**Category:** Sleep
**Source:** National Institutes of Health (NIH)

## The Four Stages of Sleep

**Stage 1 (N1) — Light Sleep:** Lasts 1–5 minutes. The transition between wakefulness and sleep. Muscles relax, heart rate slows.

**Stage 2 (N2) — Moderate Sleep:** Lasts 10–25 minutes in early cycles, longer later. Body temperature drops, eye movements stop. This stage accounts for about 50% of total sleep.

**Stage 3 (N3) — Deep Sleep:** Also called slow-wave sleep. Lasts 20–40 minutes in early cycles. This is the most restorative stage — tissue repair, immune strengthening, and growth hormone release occur here. Very difficult to wake someone during this stage.

**REM Sleep:** Rapid Eye Movement sleep begins about 90 minutes after falling asleep. Brain activity increases to near-waking levels. Dreaming occurs during REM. This stage is critical for memory consolidation, emotional processing, and learning.

## Full Cycle Duration

A complete sleep cycle takes approximately 90 minutes. Most adults experience 4–6 full cycles per night. Waking up at the end of a cycle (rather than mid-cycle) helps you feel more refreshed.

## Tips for Cycle Optimization

- Count backward in 90-minute blocks from your desired wake time to find an ideal bedtime
- Avoid alarm clocks that wake you during deep sleep — try sleep-tracking apps or sunrise alarm clocks
- Naps should be either short (20 minutes, Stage 2 only) or a full cycle (90 minutes) to avoid grogginess
