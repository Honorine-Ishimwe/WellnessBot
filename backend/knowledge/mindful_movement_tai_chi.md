# Mindful Movement and Tai Chi

**Category:** Activity
**Source:** NIH NCCIH, Harvard Health

## What Is Mindful Movement?

Mindful movement combines physical exercise with present-moment awareness. Instead of exercising on autopilot, you pay full attention to your body's sensations, breath, and movements. Tai chi, qigong, and mindful walking are prime examples.

## Tai Chi

Tai chi is an ancient Chinese practice involving slow, flowing movements coordinated with deep breathing. Often called "meditation in motion," it is gentle enough for all fitness levels and ages.

**Benefits supported by research:**
- Improves balance and reduces fall risk (especially in older adults)
- Reduces symptoms of anxiety and depression
- Lowers blood pressure
- Improves sleep quality
- Reduces chronic pain (arthritis, fibromyalgia, back pain)
- Enhances cognitive function

**Getting started:**
- Look for beginner tai chi classes at community centers, gyms, or online
- Start with 15–20 minutes, 2–3 times per week
- No special equipment needed — wear comfortable clothing and flat shoes
- Can be practiced indoors or outdoors

## Qigong

Similar to tai chi but with simpler, more repetitive movements. Combines slow movement, breathing, and meditation. Easier to learn for complete beginners.

## Mindful Walking

Walk at a slower-than-normal pace. Focus on the sensation of each foot lifting, moving through the air, and making contact with the ground. Notice your weight shifting from one foot to the other. Practice for 5–10 minutes in a quiet space.
