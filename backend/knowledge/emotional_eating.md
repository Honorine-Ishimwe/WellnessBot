# Emotional Eating: Understanding and Managing

**Category:** Nutrition
**Source:** Harvard Health, Cleveland Clinic

## What Is Emotional Eating?

Emotional eating is using food to manage feelings rather than to satisfy physical hunger. It is a common coping mechanism and is not a character flaw. Understanding the difference between emotional and physical hunger is the first step toward managing it.

## Emotional Hunger vs. Physical Hunger

**Emotional Hunger:**
- Comes on suddenly
- Craves specific comfort foods (usually salty, sweet, or high-fat)
- Feels urgent and demands immediate satisfaction
- Persists even after you are physically full
- Often followed by guilt or shame
- Located "above the neck" (mouth and mind)

**Physical Hunger:**
- Builds gradually
- Open to various food options
- Can wait (uncomfortable but manageable)
- Stops when full
- Does not produce guilt
- Located in the stomach (growling, emptiness)

## Strategies for Managing Emotional Eating

**Pause and Identify:** Before eating, ask: "Am I physically hungry, or am I trying to soothe an emotion?" Name the feeling (bored, stressed, lonely, sad, anxious).

**Alternative Coping Menu:** Create a list of non-food activities for different emotions: bored (call a friend), stressed (take a walk), lonely (text someone), sad (journal or listen to music), anxious (breathing exercises).

**Mindful Eating:** When you do eat, remove distractions. Eat slowly. Notice flavors and textures. This naturally reduces overconsumption.

**No Restriction:** Severely restricting foods increases cravings and binge episodes. Allow yourself to enjoy treats in reasonable amounts without guilt.

**Self-Compassion:** If you emotionally eat, respond with kindness rather than criticism. Shame perpetuates the cycle. Acknowledge it, learn from it, and move forward.
