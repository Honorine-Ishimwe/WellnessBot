# Dietary Fiber: Benefits and Sources

**Category:** Nutrition
**Source:** USDA Dietary Guidelines, Mayo Clinic

## What Is Dietary Fiber?

Dietary fiber is the part of plant foods that your body cannot digest. Unlike fats, proteins, and carbohydrates, fiber passes through the digestive system relatively intact. There are two types:

**Soluble Fiber:** Dissolves in water to form a gel-like substance. Helps lower blood cholesterol and glucose levels. Found in oats, beans, apples, citrus fruits, carrots, barley, and psyllium.

**Insoluble Fiber:** Promotes movement through the digestive system and adds bulk. Beneficial for those who struggle with constipation or irregular bowel movements. Found in whole wheat flour, wheat bran, nuts, beans, and vegetables like cauliflower and green beans.

## Daily Recommendations

- Women: 21–25 grams per day
- Men: 30–38 grams per day
- Most Americans consume only 15 grams per day on average

## High-Fiber Foods

- Lentils (1 cup cooked): 15.6g fiber
- Black beans (1 cup cooked): 15g fiber
- Avocado (1 medium): 10g fiber
- Raspberries (1 cup): 8g fiber
- Oatmeal (1 cup cooked): 4g fiber
- Broccoli (1 cup cooked): 5g fiber
- Chia seeds (2 tablespoons): 10g fiber
- Almonds (1 oz / 23 almonds): 3.5g fiber
- Sweet potato (1 medium, with skin): 4g fiber

## Tips for Increasing Fiber

- Increase fiber intake gradually to avoid bloating and gas
- Drink plenty of water as you increase fiber (fiber absorbs water)
- Choose whole fruits over fruit juices
- Leave edible skins on fruits and vegetables
- Swap white rice for brown rice or quinoa
- Add beans or lentils to soups, salads, and stews
