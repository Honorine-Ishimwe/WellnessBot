# Meal Planning Basics

**Category:** Nutrition
**Source:** Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics

## Benefits of Meal Planning

- Saves time and reduces daily decision fatigue
- Reduces food waste by buying only what you need
- Supports healthier eating by reducing impulse choices
- Saves money by minimizing takeout and unplanned grocery purchases
- Ensures nutritional balance across the week

## How to Start Meal Planning

**Step 1 — Choose Your Planning Window:** Start with planning 3–4 dinners per week. You do not need to plan every meal immediately.

**Step 2 — Inventory Check:** Look at what you already have in your pantry, fridge, and freezer before making a grocery list.

**Step 3 — Build Around Protein:** Choose 3–4 protein sources for the week (chicken, fish, beans, tofu), then build meals around them with vegetables and grains.

**Step 4 — Prep Ahead:** On a designated day (often Sunday), wash and chop vegetables, cook grains, marinate proteins, and portion snacks. Even 30 minutes of prep saves hours during the week.

**Step 5 — Embrace Leftovers:** Cook larger batches intentionally. Monday's roasted chicken becomes Tuesday's chicken salad. Wednesday's rice becomes Thursday's stir-fry.

## Flexible Framework (Not Rigid Rules)

- Monday: Sheet pan meal (protein + vegetables roasted together)
- Tuesday: Soup or stew (batch cook for leftovers)
- Wednesday: Stir-fry or skillet meal
- Thursday: Slow cooker or Instant Pot meal
- Friday: Simple or fun meal (homemade pizza, tacos, breakfast for dinner)
- Weekend: Cook one new recipe + eat leftovers or eat out

## Grocery Shopping Tips

- Never shop hungry — eat a snack before going
- Stick to the perimeter of the store (produce, protein, dairy) before aisles
- Read nutrition labels — check serving size, added sugars, and sodium
- Buy frozen fruits and vegetables — they are nutritionally equivalent to fresh and last longer
