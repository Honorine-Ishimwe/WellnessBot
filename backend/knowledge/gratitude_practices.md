# Gratitude Practices for Mental Wellness

**Category:** Mindfulness
**Source:** Greater Good Science Center (UC Berkeley)

## The Science of Gratitude

Research consistently shows that practicing gratitude improves psychological well-being. Studies demonstrate that people who regularly express gratitude experience fewer symptoms of depression, stronger immune function, better sleep quality, and more satisfying relationships.

## Gratitude Journaling

**The Three Good Things Exercise:**
Every evening, write down three good things that happened during the day and why they happened. They can be small ("I had a delicious lunch") or significant ("I got positive feedback at work"). The key is consistency — doing this daily for two weeks produces measurable improvements in well-being.

**Gratitude Letter:**
Write a detailed letter to someone who positively impacted your life but whom you never properly thanked. Describe what they did, how it affected you, and what it means to you now. Reading the letter aloud to them amplifies the positive effects for both people.

## Quick Daily Practices

- **Morning gratitude:** Before checking your phone, think of one thing you are grateful for today
- **Gratitude pause:** Before meals, take a moment to appreciate the food and the people who made it possible
- **Reframing:** When something goes wrong, ask "What can I learn from this?" or "What is still going right?"
- **Gratitude jar:** Write one grateful moment on a slip of paper daily. Read them all at the end of the month

## Making Gratitude Authentic

Gratitude practice should never feel forced or dismissive of real pain. It is not about pretending everything is fine. Instead, it is about widening your lens to include both challenges and blessings simultaneously.
