# Journaling for Mental Health

**Category:** Mindfulness
**Source:** University of Rochester Medical Center, APA

## Benefits of Journaling

- Reduces anxiety by externalizing worries from the mind onto paper
- Helps identify patterns in mood, triggers, and behaviors
- Improves emotional processing and self-awareness
- Strengthens immune function (studies show expressive writing improves immune markers)
- Enhances problem-solving by clarifying thoughts

## Journaling Methods

**Free Writing:** Set a timer for 10–15 minutes. Write continuously without worrying about grammar, spelling, or structure. Let thoughts flow without censorship.

**Structured Prompts:**
- "What am I feeling right now, and why?"
- "What is one thing I did well today?"
- "What is worrying me, and what is within my control?"
- "What would I tell a friend in my situation?"
- "What am I looking forward to tomorrow?"

**Bullet Journaling:** Use short, rapid-fire entries. Track daily habits (water intake, exercise, mood rating 1–10, sleep hours) alongside brief reflections.

**Worry Dump:** Before bed, write down every worry on your mind. Close the journal. This signals to your brain that the worries have been "handled" for now, reducing nighttime rumination.

## Getting Started

- Choose physical paper or a digital app — whatever feels most natural
- Write at the same time daily (morning or evening works best)
- Start with just 5 minutes
- There is no wrong way to journal — skip days without guilt
- Keep your journal private to ensure full honesty
