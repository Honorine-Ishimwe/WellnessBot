# Understanding and Managing Burnout

**Category:** Other
**Source:** World Health Organization, Mayo Clinic

## What Is Burnout?

The WHO defines burnout as a syndrome resulting from chronic workplace stress that has not been successfully managed. It is characterized by three dimensions:

**Emotional Exhaustion:** Feeling drained, overwhelmed, and unable to cope. Even small tasks feel monumental.

**Depersonalization / Cynicism:** Feeling detached from your work and the people around you. Increasing negativity, sarcasm, or indifference.

**Reduced Personal Accomplishment:** Feeling ineffective, doubting your abilities, and experiencing a decline in productivity despite working the same or more hours.

## Warning Signs

- Dreading work consistently (not just occasional bad days)
- Physical symptoms: chronic fatigue, insomnia, frequent illness, headaches
- Emotional symptoms: hopelessness, irritability, detachment, loss of motivation
- Behavioral symptoms: isolation, neglecting responsibilities, decreased performance
- Inability to disconnect from work mentally, even during time off

## Recovery Strategies

**Immediate Relief:**
- Take any available time off (even a single mental health day)
- Identify and eliminate one unnecessary commitment this week
- Talk to someone you trust about how you are feeling

**Medium-Term Recovery:**
- Set firm boundaries around work hours
- Rediscover activities that bring joy outside of work
- Prioritize sleep, nutrition, and movement
- Practice saying no to non-essential requests
- Seek professional support (therapist, counselor, or coach)

**Prevention:**
- Build regular rest into your schedule (not just vacations)
- Maintain hobbies and relationships outside of work
- Regularly assess whether your workload is sustainable
- Advocate for yourself when expectations become unreasonable

## Burnout vs. Depression

Burnout is context-specific (usually work-related) and improves with rest and boundary changes. Depression is pervasive across all areas of life and typically requires professional treatment. They can co-occur.
