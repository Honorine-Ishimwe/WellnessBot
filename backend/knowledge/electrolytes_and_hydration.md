# Electrolytes and Hydration Balance

**Category:** Hydration
**Source:** Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health

## What Are Electrolytes?

Electrolytes are minerals that carry an electrical charge in your body. The key electrolytes involved in hydration are sodium, potassium, magnesium, calcium, and chloride. They regulate fluid balance, muscle contractions, nerve signaling, and pH levels.

## When Plain Water Isn't Enough

During activities lasting longer than 60 minutes (especially in heat), plain water alone may not adequately replace lost electrolytes. Signs of electrolyte imbalance include muscle cramping, nausea, confusion, and irregular heartbeat.

## Natural Electrolyte Sources

- **Sodium:** Table salt, pickles, olives, broth
- **Potassium:** Bananas, sweet potatoes, spinach, avocados, coconut water
- **Magnesium:** Nuts (almonds, cashews), seeds (pumpkin, sunflower), dark chocolate, leafy greens
- **Calcium:** Dairy products, fortified plant milks, sardines, tofu

## Homemade Electrolyte Drink

- 2 cups water
- 1/4 teaspoon salt
- 2 tablespoons honey or maple syrup
- Juice of half a lemon or lime
- Optional: a splash of orange juice for potassium

## When to Use Commercial Sports Drinks

Sports drinks are specifically formulated for heavy sweating. They are beneficial during intense exercise exceeding 60–90 minutes, recovery from illness with vomiting or diarrhea, or working outdoors in extreme heat. For everyday hydration, water is sufficient and preferred.
