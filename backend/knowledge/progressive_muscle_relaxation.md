# Progressive Muscle Relaxation (PMR)

**Category:** Mindfulness
**Source:** American Psychological Association, Mayo Clinic

## What Is PMR?

Progressive Muscle Relaxation is a technique developed by Dr. Edmund Jacobson in the 1930s. It involves systematically tensing and then relaxing specific muscle groups throughout the body. The contrast between tension and relaxation teaches the body to recognize and release physical stress.

## Step-by-Step Guide (15–20 minutes)

Find a comfortable position (lying down or seated). Close your eyes. Take three slow, deep breaths.

**Feet:** Curl your toes tightly. Hold for 5 seconds. Release and notice the warmth and relaxation for 10–15 seconds.

**Calves:** Point your toes toward your shins, tightening calf muscles. Hold 5 seconds. Release.

**Thighs:** Squeeze your thigh muscles. Hold 5 seconds. Release.

**Glutes:** Tighten your buttock muscles. Hold 5 seconds. Release.

**Abdomen:** Pull your belly button toward your spine. Hold 5 seconds. Release.

**Hands:** Make tight fists. Hold 5 seconds. Release. Spread fingers wide.

**Arms:** Flex your biceps (make a muscle). Hold 5 seconds. Release.

**Shoulders:** Raise shoulders toward your ears. Hold 5 seconds. Drop them down.

**Neck:** Gently tilt head forward, stretching the back of your neck. Hold 5 seconds. Return to neutral.

**Face:** Scrunch all facial muscles (eyes, nose, mouth). Hold 5 seconds. Release. Relax jaw, letting mouth fall slightly open.

## Benefits

- Reduces physical tension and chronic muscle pain
- Lowers anxiety and stress-related symptoms
- Improves sleep quality (especially when done before bed)
- Helps manage headaches and migraines
- Decreases heart rate and blood pressure

## Tips for Best Results

- Practice daily for two weeks to build the habit
- The more you practice, the faster you can achieve relaxation
- Eventually you can skip the tension phase and simply "scan and release"
