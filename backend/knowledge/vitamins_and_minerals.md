# Vitamins and Minerals Essentials

**Category:** Nutrition
**Source:** NIH Office of Dietary Supplements, USDA

## Key Vitamins for Wellness

**Vitamin D (The Sunshine Vitamin):** Supports bone health, immune function, and mood. Most people are deficient. Sources: sunlight (15–30 min/day), fatty fish, fortified milk, egg yolks. Consider supplementation if blood levels are low.

**Vitamin C:** Supports immune function, skin health (collagen production), and acts as an antioxidant. Sources: citrus fruits, strawberries, bell peppers, broccoli, kiwi.

**B Vitamins (B6, B12, Folate):** Essential for energy production, brain function, and red blood cell formation. Sources: whole grains, meat, eggs, legumes, leafy greens. B12 is primarily found in animal products — vegans should supplement.

**Vitamin A:** Supports vision, immune function, and skin health. Sources: sweet potatoes, carrots, spinach, liver, eggs.

## Key Minerals

**Iron:** Carries oxygen in the blood. Deficiency causes fatigue. Sources: red meat, spinach, lentils, fortified cereals. Pair with vitamin C for better absorption.

**Magnesium:** Involved in 300+ enzyme reactions. Supports muscle function, sleep, and stress management. Sources: nuts, seeds, dark chocolate, avocados, whole grains. Many adults are deficient.

**Calcium:** Essential for bone and teeth health. Sources: dairy, fortified plant milks, leafy greens, sardines, tofu.

**Zinc:** Supports immune function and wound healing. Sources: meat, shellfish, legumes, nuts, seeds.

**Potassium:** Regulates fluid balance, muscle contractions, and blood pressure. Sources: bananas, potatoes, spinach, beans, avocados.

## Food First Approach

Aim to get nutrients from whole foods whenever possible. Supplements should fill specific gaps, not replace a balanced diet. Consult a healthcare provider before starting any supplementation regimen.
