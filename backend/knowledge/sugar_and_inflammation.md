# Sugar, Processed Foods, and Inflammation

**Category:** Nutrition
**Source:** Harvard Health, American Heart Association

## Added Sugar: The Facts

The American Heart Association recommends limiting added sugar to 25 grams (6 teaspoons) per day for women and 36 grams (9 teaspoons) for men. The average American consumes approximately 77 grams (19 teaspoons) daily.

## How Excess Sugar Affects the Body

- Causes rapid blood sugar spikes followed by crashes (energy roller coaster)
- Promotes chronic low-grade inflammation throughout the body
- Contributes to weight gain, especially visceral fat around organs
- Increases risk of type 2 diabetes, heart disease, and fatty liver disease
- Feeds harmful gut bacteria, disrupting microbiome balance
- Accelerates skin aging through a process called glycation

## Hidden Sources of Added Sugar

- Flavored yogurts (up to 20g per serving)
- Granola bars and protein bars
- Pasta sauces and ketchup
- Salad dressings
- Bread and cereals marketed as "healthy"
- Fruit juices and smoothies
- Coffee drinks from cafes

## Reading Labels

Added sugars appear under many names: high fructose corn syrup, cane sugar, dextrose, maltose, agave nectar, honey, maple syrup, and coconut sugar. Check the "Added Sugars" line on nutrition labels.

## Reducing Sugar Gradually

- Swap sugary drinks for water, sparkling water, or herbal tea
- Choose plain yogurt and add fresh fruit
- Reduce sugar in recipes by 25–50% (often unnoticeable)
- Replace candy with dark chocolate (70%+ cacao)
- Read labels and choose products with less than 5g added sugar per serving
- Allow occasional treats without guilt — complete restriction often backfires
