# SMART Goal Setting for Wellness

**Category:** Activity
**Source:** American Council on Exercise, Psychology Today

## What Are SMART Goals?

SMART is a framework for creating clear, achievable goals:

**S — Specific:** Define exactly what you want to accomplish. Instead of "exercise more," say "walk for 30 minutes during my lunch break."

**M — Measurable:** Include a number or metric so you can track progress. "Drink 8 glasses of water daily" is measurable. "Drink more water" is not.

**A — Achievable:** The goal should challenge you but remain realistic given your current situation, schedule, and resources.

**R — Relevant:** The goal should align with your broader wellness priorities and values. Ask: "Why does this matter to me?"

**T — Time-Bound:** Set a deadline or timeframe. "By the end of this month" or "for the next 30 days" creates urgency and accountability.

## Examples of SMART Wellness Goals

- "I will walk 7,000 steps every day for the next 4 weeks, tracking with my phone's step counter."
- "I will meditate for 5 minutes every morning before checking my phone for the next 21 days."
- "I will drink 8 glasses of water daily for 2 weeks, using a marked water bottle to track."
- "I will go to bed by 10:30 PM on weeknights for the next month."
- "I will eat at least 3 servings of vegetables daily for the next 3 weeks."

## Tips for Goal Success

- Write your goal down and place it where you will see it daily
- Tell someone about your goal for accountability
- Track progress daily (apps, journals, calendars)
- Celebrate small milestones along the way
- If you miss a day, resume the next day without abandoning the goal
- Review and adjust goals every 2–4 weeks based on progress
