# Self-Compassion and Inner Dialogue

**Category:** Mindfulness
**Source:** Dr. Kristin Neff Research, Greater Good Science Center

## What Is Self-Compassion?

Self-compassion involves treating yourself with the same kindness, understanding, and patience you would offer a good friend going through a difficult time. Research by Dr. Kristin Neff identifies three core components:

**Self-Kindness:** Being warm and understanding toward yourself during suffering or perceived failure, rather than harshly self-critical.

**Common Humanity:** Recognizing that suffering and imperfection are part of the shared human experience. You are not alone in your struggles.

**Mindful Awareness:** Holding painful thoughts and feelings in balanced awareness rather than over-identifying with them or suppressing them.

## Practical Exercises

**Self-Compassion Break:** When you notice you are struggling:
1. Acknowledge the difficulty: "This is a moment of suffering."
2. Remember common humanity: "Suffering is part of being human."
3. Offer yourself kindness: Place a hand on your heart and say, "May I be kind to myself."

**Rewriting Your Inner Dialogue:** When you catch a harsh inner critic ("I'm so stupid"), ask: "Would I say this to my best friend?" Rephrase with compassion: "I made a mistake, and that's okay. I'm learning."

**Compassionate Letter Writing:** Write a letter to yourself from the perspective of an unconditionally loving friend. Acknowledge your pain, remind yourself of your strengths, and offer encouragement.

## Self-Compassion vs. Self-Esteem

Self-esteem often depends on external validation and comparison. Self-compassion is unconditional — it does not require you to be better than others. It remains stable during failure, making it a more reliable foundation for emotional resilience.
