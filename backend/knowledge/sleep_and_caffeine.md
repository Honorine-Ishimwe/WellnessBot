# Sleep and Caffeine

**Category:** Sleep
**Source:** Sleep Foundation, FDA

## How Caffeine Affects Sleep

Caffeine blocks adenosine receptors in the brain. Adenosine is a chemical that builds up during waking hours and promotes sleepiness. By blocking it, caffeine keeps you alert but does not eliminate the underlying sleep debt.

## Half-Life and Timing

Caffeine has a half-life of 5–6 hours. This means if you drink 200mg of caffeine at 3 PM, approximately 100mg is still active at 9 PM. The general recommendation is to stop consuming caffeine at least 6 hours before bedtime, ideally 8–10 hours.

## Daily Limits

The FDA considers 400mg of caffeine per day (roughly 4 cups of brewed coffee) safe for most healthy adults. Pregnant individuals are advised to limit intake to 200mg per day.

## Common Caffeine Content

- Brewed coffee (8 oz): 80–100mg
- Espresso (1 shot): 63mg
- Black tea (8 oz): 40–70mg
- Green tea (8 oz): 25–50mg
- Cola (12 oz): 30–40mg
- Dark chocolate (1 oz): 12mg
- Decaf coffee (8 oz): 2–15mg

## Tips for Reducing Caffeine Dependence

- Taper gradually (reduce by 25% per week to avoid withdrawal headaches)
- Replace afternoon coffee with herbal tea or water
- Address the root cause of fatigue (sleep, nutrition, hydration) rather than masking it with caffeine
