# Introduction to Mindfulness

**Category:** Mindfulness
**Source:** NIH National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (NCCIH)

## What Is Mindfulness?

Mindfulness is the practice of purposely focusing your attention on the present moment and accepting it without judgment. It involves being aware of your thoughts, feelings, bodily sensations, and surrounding environment with openness and curiosity.

## Scientific Benefits

Research shows that regular mindfulness practice can:
- Reduce symptoms of anxiety and depression
- Lower cortisol (stress hormone) levels
- Improve focus, attention span, and working memory
- Reduce chronic pain perception
- Lower blood pressure and improve heart health
- Enhance emotional regulation and resilience
- Improve sleep quality

## Simple Ways to Start

**Mindful Breathing (2 minutes):** Sit comfortably. Close your eyes. Focus entirely on the sensation of breathing — the air entering your nostrils, your chest rising, your belly expanding. When your mind wanders (it will), gently bring attention back to the breath without self-criticism.

**Mindful Eating:** Choose one meal or snack per day to eat without distractions. Notice the colors, textures, smells, and flavors. Chew slowly. This practice improves digestion and reduces overeating.

**Mindful Walking:** During a short walk, focus on the sensation of your feet touching the ground, the rhythm of your steps, the temperature of the air. Leave your phone in your pocket.

## Common Misconceptions

- Mindfulness is NOT about clearing your mind of all thoughts
- It is NOT a religious practice (though it has roots in Buddhist meditation)
- You do NOT need to sit still for hours — even 2–5 minutes is beneficial
- Having a wandering mind does NOT mean you are doing it wrong
