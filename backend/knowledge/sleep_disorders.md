# Sleep Disorders: When to Seek Help

**Category:** Sleep
**Source:** National Sleep Foundation, Mayo Clinic

## Common Sleep Disorders

**Insomnia:** Difficulty falling asleep, staying asleep, or waking too early. Affects 10–30% of adults. Acute insomnia (lasting days to weeks) is often triggered by stress or life changes. Chronic insomnia (3+ months) may require professional intervention.

**Sleep Apnea:** Repeated pauses in breathing during sleep. Symptoms include loud snoring, gasping during sleep, morning headaches, and excessive daytime sleepiness. Requires medical evaluation and treatment (often CPAP therapy).

**Restless Leg Syndrome (RLS):** An irresistible urge to move the legs, usually worse in the evening and at rest. Can significantly delay sleep onset. Treatment may include iron supplementation, lifestyle changes, or medication.

**Circadian Rhythm Disorders:** Misalignment between your internal clock and the external day-night cycle. Common in shift workers, frequent travelers (jet lag), and adolescents (delayed sleep phase).

## When to See a Doctor

- You regularly take more than 30 minutes to fall asleep
- You wake frequently during the night and cannot return to sleep
- You snore loudly or a partner observes breathing pauses
- You feel excessively sleepy during the day despite adequate sleep hours
- Sleep problems persist for more than 4 weeks despite good sleep hygiene
- Sleep issues significantly affect your mood, concentration, or daily functioning

## What a Sleep Specialist Can Offer

- Sleep study (polysomnography) to diagnose disorders
- Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia (CBT-I), which is the first-line treatment for chronic insomnia and is more effective long-term than medication
- Appropriate medication management when needed
- Circadian rhythm adjustment protocols
