# Gut Health and the Microbiome

**Category:** Nutrition
**Source:** Harvard Health, Johns Hopkins Medicine

## What Is the Gut Microbiome?

Your gut contains trillions of bacteria, fungi, and other microorganisms collectively called the gut microbiome. These organisms play critical roles in digestion, immune function, mental health (the gut-brain axis), vitamin production, and inflammation regulation.

## Foods That Support Gut Health

**Probiotics (live beneficial bacteria):**
- Yogurt (with live active cultures)
- Kefir
- Sauerkraut (unpasteurized)
- Kimchi
- Kombucha
- Miso
- Tempeh

**Prebiotics (food for beneficial bacteria):**
- Garlic and onions
- Bananas (especially slightly green)
- Asparagus
- Oats
- Apples
- Flaxseeds
- Jerusalem artichokes

**Fiber-Rich Foods:** Diverse plant fiber feeds diverse gut bacteria. Aim for 30+ different plant foods per week (fruits, vegetables, grains, nuts, seeds, herbs, and spices all count).

## Factors That Harm Gut Health

- Excessive antibiotic use (kills beneficial bacteria alongside harmful ones)
- Diets high in processed foods and added sugars
- Chronic stress (alters gut motility and microbiome composition)
- Insufficient sleep
- Excessive alcohol consumption
- Lack of dietary diversity

## The Gut-Brain Connection

The gut and brain communicate bidirectionally through the vagus nerve. Approximately 95% of serotonin (a mood-regulating neurotransmitter) is produced in the gut. Poor gut health has been linked to increased anxiety, depression, and cognitive difficulties.
