# Stretching and Flexibility

**Category:** Activity
**Source:** American College of Sports Medicine, Mayo Clinic

## Why Stretching Matters

Regular stretching improves flexibility, increases range of motion, reduces muscle stiffness, improves posture, decreases risk of injury, and enhances blood circulation to muscles.

## Types of Stretching

**Static Stretching:** Holding a stretch for 15–30 seconds without bouncing. Best performed after exercise when muscles are warm.

**Dynamic Stretching:** Moving through a range of motion repeatedly (leg swings, arm circles, walking lunges). Best before exercise as a warm-up.

**PNF Stretching (Proprioceptive Neuromuscular Facilitation):** Advanced technique involving contracting a muscle, then stretching it further. Most effective with a partner or therapist.

## Essential Daily Stretches (10 minutes)

**Neck Rolls:** Slowly roll head in a circle, 5 times each direction.

**Shoulder Shrugs:** Lift shoulders to ears, hold 3 seconds, release. Repeat 10 times.

**Chest Opener:** Clasp hands behind your back, straighten arms, lift hands slightly. Hold 20 seconds.

**Cat-Cow:** On hands and knees, alternate arching your back (cat) and dropping your belly (cow). 10 cycles.

**Seated Forward Fold:** Sit with legs extended. Hinge at hips and reach toward toes. Hold 30 seconds.

**Hip Flexor Stretch:** Kneel on one knee, push hips forward gently. Hold 30 seconds each side.

**Standing Quad Stretch:** Stand on one foot, pull opposite heel toward glutes. Hold 30 seconds each side.

**Hamstring Stretch:** Place one foot on a low surface, lean forward with a straight back. Hold 30 seconds each side.

## Stretching Guidelines

- Never stretch cold muscles — warm up with light movement first (or stretch after exercise)
- Stretch to the point of mild tension, never pain
- Breathe deeply and slowly during each stretch
- Stretch both sides equally
- Consistency matters more than intensity — daily light stretching beats weekly intense sessions
