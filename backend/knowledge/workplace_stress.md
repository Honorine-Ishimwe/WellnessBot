# Managing Workplace Stress

**Category:** Mindfulness
**Source:** American Psychological Association, WHO

## Common Sources of Workplace Stress

- Heavy workload or unrealistic deadlines
- Lack of autonomy or control over tasks
- Poor work-life balance
- Unclear expectations or conflicting demands
- Interpersonal conflicts with colleagues or managers
- Job insecurity
- Lack of recognition or growth opportunities

## Evidence-Based Coping Strategies

**Micro-Breaks:** Take 5-minute breaks every 60–90 minutes. Stand up, stretch, look out a window, or walk briefly. These short pauses restore focus and reduce accumulated tension.

**Task Prioritization:** Use the Eisenhower Matrix — categorize tasks as Urgent+Important, Important but Not Urgent, Urgent but Not Important, or Neither. Focus energy on Important tasks.

**Boundary Setting:** Define clear work hours. Avoid checking email after a set time. Communicate boundaries to colleagues respectfully but firmly.

**Desk Ergonomics:** Poor posture contributes to physical stress. Monitor at eye level, feet flat on floor, elbows at 90 degrees, and shoulders relaxed.

**Social Connection:** Brief positive interactions with colleagues (not about work) reduce stress hormones. Even a short friendly conversation at the coffee machine helps.

## Quick Stress-Relief at Your Desk

- Three rounds of box breathing (takes 48 seconds)
- Roll your shoulders backward 10 times
- Press your palms together in front of your chest for 10 seconds (isometric release)
- Look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds every 20 minutes (the 20-20-20 rule for eye strain)
- Keep a small plant on your desk — nature exposure, even minimal, reduces cortisol
