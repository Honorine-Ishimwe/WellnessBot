# Social Connection and Loneliness

**Category:** Other
**Source:** U.S. Surgeon General Advisory on Loneliness, APA

## The Health Impact of Social Connection

The U.S. Surgeon General declared loneliness a public health epidemic. Lacking social connection increases the risk of premature death as much as smoking 15 cigarettes per day. Strong social bonds are associated with longer lifespan, stronger immune function, lower rates of anxiety and depression, and faster recovery from illness.

## Signs of Loneliness

- Feeling isolated even when surrounded by people
- Difficulty making meaningful connections
- Withdrawal from social activities you previously enjoyed
- Increased reliance on social media for connection (which often worsens loneliness)
- Fatigue, difficulty sleeping, or changes in appetite

## Building and Maintaining Connections

- Schedule regular check-ins with friends or family (even a brief text counts)
- Join a group aligned with your interests (book clubs, fitness classes, volunteer organizations)
- Practice active listening in conversations — put away your phone, make eye contact, ask follow-up questions
- Be vulnerable — sharing your authentic feelings deepens relationships
- Offer help to others — acts of kindness create reciprocal bonds
- Quality over quantity — a few deep relationships are more protective than many shallow ones

## Digital Connection

Video calls are more connecting than texts. Voice calls are more connecting than video for some people. In-person interaction remains the gold standard. Use technology to facilitate in-person meetings rather than replace them.
