# Time Management for Well-Being

**Category:** Other
**Source:** American Psychological Association, Harvard Business Review

## Why Time Management Matters for Wellness

Poor time management leads to chronic stress, burnout, sleep deprivation, skipped meals, and neglected self-care. Effective time management creates space for the activities that sustain physical and mental health.

## Key Strategies

**The Eisenhower Matrix:**
- Urgent + Important: Do immediately
- Important + Not Urgent: Schedule time for these (exercise, meal prep, sleep, relationships)
- Urgent + Not Important: Delegate or minimize
- Neither: Eliminate or reduce

**Time Blocking:** Assign specific blocks of time for specific activities. Include blocks for exercise, meals, breaks, and wind-down. Treat these health blocks as non-negotiable appointments.

**The Pomodoro Technique:** Work for 25 minutes, then take a 5-minute break. After 4 cycles, take a longer 15–30 minute break. This structure prevents burnout and maintains focus.

**Batch Similar Tasks:** Group similar activities together (answering emails, making phone calls, running errands) to reduce context-switching fatigue.

**The 2-Minute Rule:** If a task takes less than 2 minutes, do it immediately. This prevents small tasks from accumulating into an overwhelming list.

## Protecting Wellness Time

- Schedule exercise, sleep, and meals before work meetings
- Learn to say no to non-essential commitments
- Build buffer time between appointments (avoid back-to-back scheduling)
- Review your week every Sunday evening — identify where wellness activities fit
- Accept that not everything will get done — prioritize what matters most
