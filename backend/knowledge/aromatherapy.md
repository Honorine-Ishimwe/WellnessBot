# Aromatherapy for Relaxation

**Category:** Other
**Source:** NIH NCCIH, Johns Hopkins Medicine

## What Is Aromatherapy?

Aromatherapy uses essential oils (concentrated plant extracts) to promote physical and emotional well-being. When inhaled, aromatic molecules interact with the olfactory system and limbic system (the brain's emotional center), influencing mood, stress levels, and relaxation.

## Evidence-Supported Essential Oils

**Lavender:** The most studied essential oil for relaxation. Research shows it reduces anxiety, improves sleep quality, and lowers heart rate. Use in a diffuser before bedtime or add a few drops to a warm bath.

**Chamomile:** Calming properties that help with sleep and anxiety. Roman chamomile is preferred for relaxation.

**Bergamot:** A citrus oil that reduces cortisol levels and improves mood. Uplifting yet calming.

**Peppermint:** Improves alertness and reduces fatigue. Helpful for headache relief when applied (diluted) to temples.

**Eucalyptus:** Supports respiratory health and mental clarity. Refreshing and invigorating.

**Ylang Ylang:** Reduces blood pressure and promotes calm. Has a sweet, floral scent.

## Safe Use Guidelines

- Never apply undiluted essential oils directly to skin (dilute with a carrier oil like coconut, jojoba, or almond oil)
- Use a diffuser for 30–60 minutes at a time, not continuously
- Keep essential oils away from eyes, ears, and mucous membranes
- Some oils are toxic to pets (especially cats) — research before diffusing around animals
- Essential oils are not regulated by the FDA — choose reputable brands
- Aromatherapy complements but does not replace medical treatment
