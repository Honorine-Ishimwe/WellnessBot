# Habit Stacking: Building New Habits

**Category:** Activity
**Source:** James Clear (Atomic Habits), BJ Fogg Research

## What Is Habit Stacking?

Habit stacking is linking a new habit to an existing one. The formula is: "After I [CURRENT HABIT], I will [NEW HABIT]." Your existing habits serve as triggers for the new behavior, making it easier to remember and execute.

## Examples

- "After I pour my morning coffee, I will meditate for 2 minutes."
- "After I sit down at my desk, I will write down my top 3 priorities."
- "After I brush my teeth at night, I will write in my gratitude journal."
- "After I eat lunch, I will take a 10-minute walk."
- "After I put on my pajamas, I will do 5 minutes of stretching."

## Why It Works

Existing habits have strong neural pathways in the brain. By attaching new behaviors to these established routines, you leverage the brain's existing automation rather than relying solely on motivation and willpower, which fluctuate daily.

## Making Habits Stick: The Four Laws

**Make it Obvious:** Put visual cues in your environment. Want to drink more water? Place a full water bottle on your desk. Want to read before bed? Put a book on your pillow.

**Make it Attractive:** Pair habits with things you enjoy. Listen to your favorite podcast only while walking. This is called temptation bundling.

**Make it Easy:** Reduce friction. Want to exercise in the morning? Sleep in your workout clothes. Want to eat healthier? Prep ingredients on Sunday.

**Make it Satisfying:** Track your streak. Use a calendar and mark each day you complete the habit. The visual progress becomes its own reward.

## The Two-Minute Rule

When starting a new habit, scale it down to two minutes or less. "Read before bed" becomes "read one page." "Run three miles" becomes "put on running shoes." Once the habit is established, gradually increase.
