# Hydrating Foods and Beverages

**Category:** Hydration
**Source:** USDA, Harvard Health

## Top Hydrating Foods (by water content)

- **Cucumber:** 96% water — refreshing snack, great in salads and infused water
- **Watermelon:** 92% water — also provides lycopene, vitamins A and C
- **Strawberries:** 91% water — rich in antioxidants and fiber
- **Celery:** 95% water — low calorie, contains potassium and vitamin K
- **Lettuce (iceberg):** 96% water — light base for salads and wraps
- **Zucchini:** 94% water — versatile in both raw and cooked dishes
- **Tomatoes:** 94% water — excellent source of lycopene and vitamin C
- **Bell peppers:** 92% water — packed with vitamin C
- **Oranges:** 87% water — natural source of electrolytes and vitamin C
- **Yogurt (plain):** 88% water — also provides protein, calcium, and probiotics

## Beverages Beyond Water

- **Herbal tea:** Caffeine-free options (chamomile, peppermint, rooibos) count toward daily fluid intake
- **Coconut water:** Natural electrolytes, lower sugar than sports drinks
- **Milk:** Contains water, protein, calcium, and electrolytes — effective for rehydration
- **Broths and soups:** Excellent hydration source, especially during illness

## Beverages That Can Dehydrate

- **Alcohol:** Acts as a diuretic, increasing urine output. Alternate alcoholic drinks with water
- **High-caffeine energy drinks:** Excessive caffeine can increase fluid loss
- **Sugary sodas:** High sugar content can slow water absorption
