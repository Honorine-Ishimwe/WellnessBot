# Managing Blue Light and Sleep

**Category:** Sleep
**Source:** Harvard Health Publishing

## How Blue Light Affects Sleep

Blue light (wavelengths of 450–495 nanometers) is emitted by smartphones, tablets, computers, LED TVs, and fluorescent lighting. Exposure to blue light in the evening suppresses melatonin production by up to 50%, delaying the onset of sleep and reducing overall sleep quality.

## Evidence-Based Strategies

**The 30-60 Rule:** Stop using screens at least 30 minutes before bed (60 minutes is ideal). Replace screen time with reading a physical book, journaling, or gentle stretching.

**Night Mode / Blue Light Filters:** Enable built-in night mode on devices (Night Shift on iOS, Night Light on Windows/Android). These shift the display to warmer tones. While helpful, they do not eliminate blue light entirely.

**Blue Light Blocking Glasses:** Amber-tinted glasses worn 2–3 hours before bed have been shown in studies to improve sleep quality and melatonin levels. Look for glasses that block at least 90% of blue light.

**Daytime Blue Light Is Beneficial:** Blue light exposure during the day boosts alertness, mood, and cognitive performance. Aim for natural sunlight exposure in the morning (15–30 minutes) to strengthen your circadian rhythm.

**Evening Lighting:** Switch to warm-toned bulbs (2700K or lower) in the evening. Salt lamps and candles emit virtually no blue light.
