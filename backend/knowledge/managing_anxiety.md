# Managing Anxiety: Practical Strategies

**Category:** Mindfulness
**Source:** Anxiety and Depression Association of America, National Institute of Mental Health

## Understanding Anxiety

Anxiety is the body's natural response to perceived threats. It becomes problematic when it is disproportionate to the situation, persists beyond the triggering event, or interferes with daily functioning. Occasional anxiety is normal — chronic or debilitating anxiety may benefit from professional support.

## Evidence-Based Self-Help Strategies

**Cognitive Reframing:** When anxious thoughts arise, challenge them: "What evidence supports this thought? What evidence contradicts it? What would I tell a friend thinking this?" This interrupts automatic catastrophizing patterns.

**Worry Time:** Designate 15–20 minutes per day as "worry time." When anxious thoughts arise outside this window, write them down and postpone them. This prevents all-day rumination while still honoring your concerns.

**Behavioral Activation:** Anxiety often leads to avoidance, which reinforces fear. Gradually expose yourself to avoided situations in small, manageable steps.

**Physical Activity:** Exercise reduces anxiety symptoms as effectively as some medications for mild-to-moderate anxiety. Even a 10-minute walk can provide relief.

**Limit Stimulants:** Caffeine can mimic and worsen anxiety symptoms (racing heart, restlessness). Reduce intake if you notice a connection.

**Sleep Hygiene:** Anxiety and poor sleep create a vicious cycle. Prioritize consistent sleep habits.

## When to Seek Professional Help

- Anxiety significantly interferes with work, relationships, or daily activities
- You experience panic attacks
- You avoid situations, places, or people due to fear
- Physical symptoms (chest tightness, chronic headaches, digestive issues) are present
- Self-help strategies have not provided sufficient relief after consistent effort

Effective professional treatments include Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT), medication, and their combination.
