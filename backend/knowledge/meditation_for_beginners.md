# Meditation for Beginners

**Category:** Mindfulness
**Source:** NIH NCCIH, Headspace Research

## Types of Meditation

**Focused Attention Meditation:** Concentrate on a single object — your breath, a candle flame, a mantra, or a sound. When the mind wanders, gently redirect attention back. This builds concentration skills.

**Body Scan Meditation:** Lie down comfortably. Starting from the top of your head, slowly move your attention through each body part. Notice sensations (tingling, warmth, tension) without trying to change them. Spend 20–30 seconds on each area.

**Loving-Kindness Meditation (Metta):** Silently repeat phrases of goodwill: "May I be happy. May I be healthy. May I be at peace." Gradually extend these wishes to loved ones, acquaintances, difficult people, and all beings.

**Open Monitoring:** Instead of focusing on one thing, observe all experiences (thoughts, sounds, sensations) as they arise without attachment. Watch them come and go like clouds passing.

## Starting a Practice

- Begin with just 2–5 minutes per day
- Choose the same time daily (morning often works best)
- Sit comfortably — you do not need to sit cross-legged on the floor
- Use guided meditations initially (apps like Insight Timer offer thousands for free)
- Increase duration gradually as the habit solidifies
- Missing a day is normal — simply resume the next day without guilt

## What to Expect

The first few sessions may feel frustrating. Your mind will wander constantly. This is completely normal and expected. The act of noticing the wandering and returning attention IS the meditation. Each return strengthens your mental muscle, similar to how each repetition at the gym strengthens a physical muscle.
