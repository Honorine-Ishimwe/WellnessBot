# Daily Hydration Guide

**Category:** Hydration
**Source:** CDC, National Academies of Sciences

## How Much Water Do You Need?

The general recommendation is approximately 3.7 liters (125 oz) per day for adult men and 2.7 liters (91 oz) per day for adult women from all beverages and food combined. About 20% of daily water intake typically comes from food.

**Practical Daily Targets (from beverages alone):**
- Women: ~9 cups (2.2 liters / 74 oz)
- Men: ~13 cups (3.0 liters / 101 oz)

## Factors That Increase Water Needs

- **Exercise:** Drink an extra 1.5–2.5 cups for short workouts. For intense exercise lasting over an hour, consider beverages with electrolytes.
- **Hot or humid weather:** Sweating increases fluid loss significantly.
- **Altitude:** Higher altitudes increase respiration rate, leading to more water vapor loss.
- **Illness:** Fever, vomiting, and diarrhea cause rapid dehydration. Oral rehydration solutions are recommended.
- **Pregnancy and breastfeeding:** Pregnant women need ~10 cups/day; breastfeeding women need ~13 cups/day.

## Simple Hydration Strategies

- Start each morning with a glass of water before coffee or breakfast
- Carry a reusable water bottle and refill it throughout the day
- Set hourly reminders on your phone as gentle nudges
- Drink a glass of water before each meal
- Flavor water with slices of cucumber, lemon, mint, or berries if plain water feels boring
